## 1. The Entity-First Principle Applied to EPTDESIGN

### What "Entity-First" Means

Search engines — and increasingly AI systems — do not merely index pages. They build *knowledge graphs*: structured representations of who an entity is, what it does, where it operates, when it was founded, and who it is connected to. Before Google can confidently rank EPTDESIGN for "landscape architecture Pasadena" or before ChatGPT can cite EPTDESIGN in response to "best landscape architects in Southern California," the entity itself must be clearly defined and corroborated across multiple sources.

Entity clarity precedes keyword strategy. A firm with ambiguous or thin entity signals will not rank well even with technically correct on-page SEO. EPTDESIGN currently has almost none of the entity infrastructure required.

### What Google and AI Need to Know About EPTDESIGN Right Now

The following facts are known internally but are either absent from or fragmented across EPTDESIGN's online presence. Each represents a gap in the knowledge graph:

| Entity Attribute | Current Online Status | Required Action |
|---|---|---|
| Founded 1962 | Not clearly stated on site | Add to About, Homepage, Schema |
| 100% employee-owned (ESOP) | Not mentioned online | ESOP story page + Schema |
| 5 studio locations (Pasadena, Irvine, Claremont, Santa Barbara, San Diego) | 5 GBP listings exist but incomplete | Expand GBP + build location pages |
| Lineage: Bob Eriksson → Garrett Eckbo | Not mentioned online | About/History page + PR |
| Named principals + credentials (FASLA, LEED AP, ASLA) | People pages exist but thin | Expand each principal page |
| Sectors served | Work filters exist; no service pages | Build /services/ architecture |
| Key clients (Kaiser, Pomona, Cedars-Sinai, Google) | Shown in portfolio, not structured | Schema + case study content |
| Awards (ASLA Merit, AIA, ENR) | Not consolidated or structured | Awards page + Schema |
| Geographic licensure (Dan Hoon, 6-state) | Not mentioned | Service/People pages |

### The Entity Clarity Mandate

Before any keyword research, content production, or link building begins, EPTDESIGN must pass what can be called the "Knowledge Panel Test": if you search "EPTDESIGN Pasadena landscape architecture," does Google return a Knowledge Panel with the firm's location, founding year, principals, and description? Currently it does not.

Passing this test requires:

1. **A complete, accurate Google Business Profile** for the Pasadena HQ with full description, services, and secondary categories
2. **Consistent NAP (Name, Address, Phone)** across all five locations and all directories
3. **Organization schema** on the homepage and About page
4. **A Wikipedia-level "about" page** on the site itself — factual, structured, link-rich, covering founding, lineage, ownership structure, and sector expertise
5. **External corroboration** — citations from ASLA, AIA, project partners (HOK, Gensler, SOM), and client institutions (UCLA, Pomona College)

Entity-first is not a metaphor. It is the prerequisite for everything else.

## 3. Byron Sharp Application: Mental Availability for Landscape Architecture in SoCal

### The Core Insight

Byron Sharp's *How Brands Grow* established that market share is won primarily through *mental availability* — the probability that a buyer thinks of a brand when entering a buying situation. For professional services, this is especially true: when a university facilities director or a developer's VP of development reaches the RFP stage, they compile a list of firms from memory and reputation, not from a Google search. The search happens to validate the shortlist, not to build it.

EPTDESIGN's SEO strategy must therefore operate on two levels simultaneously:

1. **Short-term:** Capture buyers who are actively searching (search intent targeting)
2. **Long-term:** Build mental availability so buyers think of EPTDESIGN before they search

### Building Mental Availability Through Search

Mental availability in professional services is built through:

- **Salience across multiple touchpoints:** GBP listings, project features in trade publications, principal bylines, award announcements, LinkedIn thought leadership
- **Category-entry point dominance:** When a buyer thinks "landscape architect for a university campus," EPTDESIGN should surface through Pomona College, UCLA, and Claremont references — actual project evidence that creates association
- **Consistent brand signals:** The EPTDESIGN name must appear consistently across all surfaces — site, GBP, directories, social, press — so that repeated exposure builds familiarity before the buying moment arrives

### Category-Entry Points for EPTDESIGN

A "category-entry point" is the context in which a buyer's need arises. EPTDESIGN should map its content strategy to these specific entry points:

| Buyer | Category-Entry Point | EPTDESIGN Content That Creates Association |
|---|---|---|
| Developer | "We're breaking ground on a multifamily project in OC" | Irvine Company portfolio, Dan Hoon features, /locations/irvine/ |
| University FM Director | "We need to redesign the quad for our master plan" | Pomona College case studies, /services/campus-landscape-architecture/ |
| Hospital administrator | "Our new medical building needs outdoor healing spaces" | Kaiser/Cedars-Sinai case studies, /services/healthcare-landscape-design/ |
| HNW homeowner | "We want to redesign our estate grounds" | Nord Eriksson Veranda feature, /services/estate-residential-garden-design/ |
| Municipal planner | "We're RFP-ing a riverfront park improvement" | Glendale Narrows Riverwalk, /services/parks-civic-design/ |

The goal is not just to rank for these searches. The goal is to be the firm that is already in the buyer's mind when they start the process — because they've seen the Pomona College project feature, or read Nord Eriksson's piece in Veranda, or attended a principal's ASLA chapter presentation.

### The "Memory Structure" Implication

For EPTDESIGN, building memory structures means:
- Publishing the Eckbo lineage story so that architects and planners who know Eckbo associate that tradition with EPTDESIGN
- Featuring the Veranda / Martha Stewart Living coverage so that estate buyers have a cultural reference point
- Making the Kaiser Permanente four-project relationship a visible case study so that healthcare developers associate EPTDESIGN with that sector
- Ensuring that every ASLA award announcement references EPTDESIGN by full name and includes a link to the site

## 4. Content Philosophy

### The Foundational Rule: Depth Over Breadth, Named Over Generic

EPTDESIGN should not produce generic landscape architecture blog content. "5 Tips for Sustainable Garden Design" is produced by hundreds of firms and ranks for nothing. It adds no entity signal, creates no AI citation opportunity, and builds no mental availability.

Instead, every piece of content should pass this test: **Could only EPTDESIGN have written this?**

If the answer is yes — because it features a specific project, a named principal, a verifiable credential, or a documented outcome — it is worth producing. If the answer is no, it is not worth producing.

### Content Hierarchy

**Tier 1 — Foundational (Build First)**
Content that defines the entity and cannot exist elsewhere:
- About/History page (1962 founding, Eckbo lineage, ESOP, studio expansion)
- Principal biography pages (named, credentialed, with specific project associations)
- Service pages (nine pages as specified in Pillar B)
- Location pages (five pages as specified in Pillar C)

**Tier 2 — Case Studies (Build Second)**
Project-specific content that demonstrates capability and creates citation opportunities:
- Google Venice Campus (technology sector, urban environment)
- Cedars-Sinai AHSP (healthcare, healing garden)
- Glendale Narrows Riverwalk (civic, urban waterway)
- Kaiser Permanente portfolio (four projects — healthcare system relationship)
- Pomona College portfolio (four projects — higher education relationship)
- UCLA Botanical Garden (higher education, ecological)
- Village at Irvine Spectrum (multifamily, master-planned community)

Each case study should follow a structured format:
- Client and project name (full, not abbreviated)
- Location (city, state)
- Year completed
- Project scope (acreage, program elements)
- EPTDESIGN role and principals involved
- Design challenge and approach
- Measurable outcomes where available (LEED certification, awards, acreage restored)
- Client quote if obtainable
- Related services and principal links

**Tier 3 — Thought Leadership (Build Third, Ongoing)**
Principal-authored content that builds personal authority and feeds AI citation systems:
- Nord Eriksson: Estate design philosophy, California modernist tradition, private garden principles
- Stephen Carroll: Sustainable site design, LEED in landscape architecture, healing garden evidence
- Dan Hoon: Multifamily amenity design trends, community landscape in dense housing
- Scott Horsley: Practice leadership, ESOP model in design firms, Southern California growth patterns

**What Not to Produce:**
- Generic "landscape design tips" posts
- "Top 10" listicles
- Seasonal content ("Spring Garden Ideas")
- Content that does not mention EPTDESIGN, its principals, or its specific projects

### The Voice Standard

All content should reflect the voices of named principals, not anonymous marketing copy. When a service page says "We believe healing gardens reduce patient anxiety," it is weightless. When it says "Stephen Carroll, ASLA LEED AP, has designed three Kaiser Permanente facilities using evidence-based healing garden principles developed at UC Davis Medical Center," it is citable, credible, and hard to replicate.

## 6. Search Everywhere Strategy

In 2026, "search" means at least six distinct surfaces, each with its own logic and optimization requirements. EPTDESIGN must be present on all of them — but the investment should be proportional to where EPTDESIGN's buyer actually searches.

**Landscape architecture buyer search behavior assumptions** (to be validated with Alison Spicer):

- Developers search via Google organic + LinkedIn + peer referral
- HNW residential clients search via Google organic + Houzz + editorial referral (Veranda, AD)

### Surface-by-Surface Strategy

**1. Google Organic Search**
Primary surface. The service pages and case studies built under Pillars B and D are the core investment. Target: 50+ ranking keywords within 12 months, 200+ within 24 months.

Google's AI-generated answer boxes that appear for informational queries. To be cited in AI Overviews, content must be: authoritative, structured, factually specific, and well-linked. Service pages with FAQ schema, and case studies with measurable outcomes, are the primary candidates. Target: 3–5 AI Overview appearances within 12 months for sector-specific queries.

**4. ChatGPT / Perplexity Citations**
These AI systems are trained on web data and will cite sources that appear authoritative and accessible. Unblocking AI crawlers (Pillar D) is the prerequisite. The Eckbo lineage story, FASLA fellowship content, and LEED Platinum case studies are the highest-probability citation candidates. Target: appearance in AI responses to "landscape architecture firms Southern California" queries within 18 months.

**5. LinkedIn Thought Leadership**
LinkedIn content from principal accounts is indexed by Google, cited by AI systems, and read directly by institutional buyers. A bimonthly publishing cadence — one article per principal per quarter — creates a compound library of credentialed, named expert content. **6. Houzz Pro**
The primary residential discovery platform for landscape architects. Nord Eriksson's estate residential work is directly addressable here. A complete Houzz Pro profile with 50+ project photos, awards, and client reviews creates a parallel discovery path for HNW residential buyers. Target: Houzz Pro "Best of" badge within 18 months.

The most durable SEO strategy is one built on content that competitors cannot replicate. EPTDESIGN has six irreplicable assets. Competitors may have some of these; no competitor has all of them combined.

**Asset 2: The Garrett Eckbo Lineage**

Connecting EPTDESIGN's lineage to Eckbo — through Bob Eriksson's history — gives the firm an intellectual heritage that almost no Southern California competitor can claim.
