## Executive Summary

Before committing resources to building service pages for all five market sectors, this brief provides a systematic research framework to validate **where your prospective clients actually search** for landscape architects.

The goal is simple: **Prioritize SEO investment in sectors where clients discover firms through search, not just RFPs and relationships.**

## Research Questions to Answer

For each of the five market sectors, we need to determine:

1. **Do clients search for this service?** (Search volume exists)
2. **Is the search commercially valuable?** (High-intent queries, not just informational)
3. **Can we compete?** (Who ranks now, and what's their Domain Rating?)
4. **What's the competitive landscape?** (Are our direct competitors visible, or is it wide open?)

## How to Conduct This Research (Step-by-Step)

### Tools You'll Need
1. **Google Search** (incognito mode)
2. **Google Keyword Planner** (free with Google Ads account) OR **Ahrefs Keywords Explorer** (if you have access)
3. **ChatGPT** or **Perplexity AI** (to test AI citation behavior)
4. **Spreadsheet** (to track findings)

### Step 1: Search Volume Research (30 minutes)

For each query listed above:

1. **Google Keyword Planner:** Enter the query and check "Avg. monthly searches"
   - **High volume:** 100+ searches/month
   - **Moderate volume:** 10-100 searches/month
   - **Low volume:** <10 searches/month

2. **Note related keywords** that Google suggests — these reveal what clients are actually searching

3. **Record in spreadsheet:**
   | Query | Monthly Volume | Related Queries | Commercial Intent (Y/N) |
   |-------|----------------|-----------------|------------------------|

### Step 2: Competitor SERP Analysis (30 minutes)

For each query:

1. **Search in Google (incognito mode)**
2. **Record who ranks in positions 1-5:**
   - Are they direct competitors (landscape architecture firms)?
   - Are they directories (Houzz, Yelp, Thumbtack)?
   - Are they project owners (universities, hospitals)?
3. **Check their Domain Rating** (use Ahrefs Site Explorer or Moz Link Explorer)
4. **Ask:** "If they can rank, can we?"

### Step 3: Project Attribution Test (20 minutes)

For EPT's named clients, search:
- "Kaiser Permanente landscape architect"
- "Pomona College landscape design"
- "Cedars-Sinai landscape architect"
- "UCLA Botanical Garden landscape architect"
- "Glendale Narrows Riverwalk landscape architect"

**Record:**
- Does EPTDESIGN appear anywhere on page 1?
- Who ranks instead? (AHBE? Studio-MLA? UCLA itself?)
- Is project attribution recoverable with content investment?

### Step 4: AI Citation Test (20 minutes)

Ask ChatGPT and Perplexity:

1. "Who are the best landscape architecture firms for estate residential design in Southern California?"
2. "Which landscape architects specialize in healthcare campus design in California?"
3. "What are the top landscape architecture firms for university campus design on the West Coast?"
4. "Who are leading landscape architects for multifamily housing in Orange County?"
5. "Which firms specialize in public parks and civic landscape architecture in Los Angeles?"

**Record:**
- Does EPTDESIGN appear in ANY responses?
- Which competitors are cited?
- What language/credentials do AI systems prioritize?

### Scoring Criteria

- 5 = High volume (100+ searches/month for core queries)
- 3 = Moderate volume (10-100 searches/month)
- 1 = Low/no volume (<10 searches/month)

**Commercial Intent** (1-5)
- 5 = Direct hiring intent (e.g., "landscape architect Pasadena estate")
- 3 = Research phase (e.g., "landscape architecture firms Southern California")
- 1 = Informational only (e.g., "what is landscape architecture")

**Competitive Landscape** (1-5)
- 5 = Wide open, few competitors ranking, low DR sites
- 3 = Moderate competition, regional firms ranking, DR 30-50
- 1 = Dominated by national firms, DR 60+, hard to compete

**Project Attribution** (1-5)
- 5 = EPT's projects are searchable and attributable

- 1 = Zero project visibility, impossible to recover

**AI Citation Potential** (1-5)
- 5 = Sector-specific credentials (FASLA, LEED AP) are searchable, AI cites firms in this category
- 3 = Some citations exist but dominated by national firms
- 1 = Zero AI citations, no pathway to inclusion
