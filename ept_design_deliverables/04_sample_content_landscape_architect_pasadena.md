## A Lineage That Shapes How We Work

Landscape architecture has a living intellectual tradition in California. EPTDESIGN is part of it.

Our founder, Bob Eriksson, trained directly under Garrett Eckbo — one of the defining figures of California modernist landscape design and a practitioner who believed that landscape architecture should serve people, not simply decorate property. Eckbo's influence on the California landscape movement is well-documented. His insistence on integrating human use, ecological thinking, and clear spatial logic into every design decision shaped an entire generation of practitioners — including those who built this firm.

That lineage is not a footnote in our history. It is the reason EPTDESIGN approaches each project by asking first how people will move through, inhabit, and experience a place — and only then how it will look. [Internal link: anchor text "our history and founding" → `/about/history/`]

Sixty-four years of continuous practice in Southern California means we have seen trends arrive and depart. We have watched materials fall in and out of favour. We have observed which design decisions age well and which do not. That experience shapes every project we take on — whether it is an estate garden in San Marino or a healthcare campus in Anaheim.

## Why Pasadena — And Why It Matters to Our Work

Pasadena is not a generic Southern California suburb. It is a city with a specific character: a strong tradition of Craftsman and Spanish Colonial Revival residential architecture, an unusually high concentration of estate-scale residential properties, a distinguished institutional landscape in the Caltech and Huntington Library corridors, and a civic culture that has historically invested in public space.

EPTDESIGN has been embedded in this context for six decades. We know the soils. We know the microclimates. We know which plant communities perform under Pasadena's specific conditions — the summer heat, the occasional frost, the Santa Ana wind events, and the water restrictions that have reshaped residential landscape practice across the region.

That local knowledge is not a small thing. It affects which plant species we specify, how we design for irrigation efficiency, which contractors we trust for specific scopes of work, and how we navigate the City of Pasadena's permitting and plan check processes. It is the accumulated advantage of doing the same work in the same geography for more than sixty years.
