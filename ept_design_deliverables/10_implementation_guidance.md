### 5.2 Schema Markup Requirements

Implement the following schema types. Do not use plugins that generate generic schema — implement the specific JSON-LD blocks provided in each content page draft.

**LocalBusiness schema** — Required on all location pages (/services/landscape-architect-[city]/)
- Include: name, address, telephone, geo coordinates, openingHoursSpecification, sameAs (GBP URL), areaServed, serviceType, award, foundingDate
- Do NOT use aggregateRating unless you have a reliable automated mechanism to keep it current

**FAQPage schema** — Required on all service and location pages with an FAQ section
- Match FAQ schema exactly to the visible on-page FAQ content
- Do not include FAQ items in schema that are not visible on the page

**BreadcrumbList schema** — Required on all interior pages
- Example for Pasadena page: Home > Services > Landscape Architect Pasadena

**Organization schema** — Add to homepage and /about/ page
- Include: name, url, logo, foundingDate, numberOfEmployees, sameAs (all social profiles), address (HQ)

**Test all schema** using Google's Rich Results Test (https://search.google.com/test/rich-results) before each page launches.

### 5.4 Sitemap Requirements

- Generate and submit an XML sitemap to Google Search Console
- Include all published service pages, location pages, project/work pages, and people pages
- Exclude: thank-you pages, admin pages, search result pages, paginated archive pages beyond page 1
- Set `<priority>` values: homepage = 1.0, service pages = 0.8, project pages = 0.6, blog/news = 0.5
- Set `<changefreq>` to "monthly" for service pages, "yearly" for static pages
- Re-submit sitemap to GSC every time a new page is published

### 5.5 Additional Technical Requirements

- **Page speed:** Target 75+ on Google PageSpeed Insights (mobile). Compress all images; use next-gen formats (WebP). Lazy-load below-fold images.
- **Mobile:** All new pages must render correctly on iOS Safari and Android Chrome at 375px width.
- **Canonical tags:** Add `<link rel="canonical">` to all pages. Confirm no duplicate content exists between www and non-www versions.
- **HTTPS:** Confirm all URLs return HTTPS. Fix any mixed content warnings.
- **404 monitoring:** Set up GSC coverage report alerts. Resolve any 404 errors on crawled pages.

## Section 7: Review Generation Programme

### 7.1 When to Ask for a Review

**Optimal ask moments:**
1. **Project completion** — within 2 weeks of final handover or site completion sign-off
2. **Six-month follow-up** — return email to the same contact six months after project completion (gardens and landscapes change with seasons; a 6-month follow-up often generates warmer reviews)
3. **After a positive interaction** — if a client sends unsolicited positive feedback by email, reply with a direct link to leave a Google review

**Do not ask:**
- During active construction (too much is still uncertain)
- Immediately after a scope dispute or billing discussion
- From clients who have expressed any dissatisfaction without first resolving it

## Section 8: Common Implementation Mistakes to Avoid

These are the errors that most commonly derail professional services SEO programmes. They are included here because they are all avoidable with planning.

| Mistake | Why It Matters | How to Avoid It |
|---|---|---|
| **Publishing content before AI crawlers are unblocked** | Content published while GPTBot and Claude-Web are blocked will not be indexed by AI systems. You will build pages that cannot influence AI citation answers. | Complete Cloudflare unblocking (Checklist items 1–4) before publishing any new page. Verify with robots.txt check. |
| **Ignoring GBP while building service pages** | GBP drives calls and local pack rankings faster than organic search. Neglecting it in favour of content production leaves the fastest channel untouched. | GBP optimisation is Phase 0 Week 1. It runs in parallel with, not after, content development. |
| **NAP inconsistency across listings** | If the firm's name, address, or phone number appears differently on GBP, the website, and directories, Google reduces trust in all listings. | Standardise NAP before any directory submissions. Document the canonical version and use it everywhere. |
| **Launching service pages with broken internal links** | Service pages with no internal links from the rest of the site are effectively invisible — Google discovers them slowly if at all. | Add internal links from the homepage, /work/, and /about/ to every new service page before or on the day it launches. |
| **Using unsupported project outcome claims in content** | Claims about project outcomes that cannot be verified by a named principal or a published source create liability and undermine the authoritative tone. | Every factual claim on a service page must pass through the Content Completion Checklist before publishing. No exceptions. |
| **Treating review generation as a one-off campaign** | A burst of 10 reviews followed by silence looks unnatural and quickly becomes stale. Google rewards consistent, ongoing review activity. | Schedule review outreach as a recurring calendar task tied to project completion, not a quarterly campaign. |
| **Asking all clients to review the same GBP listing** | Reviews concentrated on one location listing do not help the other four studios rank in their local markets. | Direct each client to the GBP listing of the studio that managed their project. |
| **Building schema but not testing it** | Schema markup errors (mismatched text, broken JSON) mean Google ignores the structured data. | Run every page through Google Rich Results Test before launch. Fix any errors before publishing. |
| **Reporting in SEO metrics, not business outcomes** | Principals will not allocate ongoing marketing budget to support "organic impressions" or "keyword rankings." They will support "inbound project enquiries" and "GBP calls." | Use the Quarterly Report template from the Measurement Framework. Never present raw SEO data to principals without translating it to business outcomes first. |
| **Delaying the programme because content is not "perfect"** | Imperfect published content outranks perfect unpublished content. A service page with one placeholder resolved and a live schema block begins accruing signals immediately. | Resolve all REQUIRED checklist items (confirmed facts, no false claims) and publish. Refine iteratively. Perfectionism is the most common reason professional services SEO stalls. |
